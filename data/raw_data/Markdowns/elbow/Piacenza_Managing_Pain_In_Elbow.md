### Systematic review 

## Effectiveness of conservative treatment in the management of post-traumatic elbow stiffness: A systematic review

##### A B S T R A C T 

_Keywords:_ Rehabilitation Elbow stiffness Conservative treatment Posttraumatic Physiotherapy 

_Background:_ Post-traumatic elbow stiffness is a common consequence following trauma or surgery, resulting in significant limb disability, with a negative impact on daily life. Although conservative treatment is the first-line approach, it is not yet known which is most suitable and effective. 

_Objective:_ To investigate the effectiveness of conservative treatments in patients with post-traumatic elbow stiffness. 

_Method:_ A protocol for this systematic review was published in PROSPERO (CRD42024517823). PRISMA standards were followed. An extensive systematic search was conducted in six databases (PubMed, CINHAL, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Scopus, and PEDro), and the CENTRAL trial register. Two reviewers independently assessed, selected results, collected data, rated the risk of bias (RoB) of included studies with the Cochrane risk of bias tool, synthesized the available evidence, and rated it using GRADE methodology. _Results:_ Five studies were included in the review, although high variability in interventions and comparators precluded the synthesis of results into a meta-analysis. Large effect sizes were observed when conservative treatment was initiated immediately after immobilization, improving elbow functionality (SMD 3.07; 95%CI 1.91 to 4.23), and pain (SMD 1.83; 95%CI 0.91 to 2.76). Results indicate that Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation (SMD = − 1.22; 95%CI [-1.90, − 0.54]) and Graded Motor Imagery (SMD = − 2.79; 95%CI [-3.59, − 2.00]) were more effective than comparisons in recovering elbow functionality and pain reduction. _Conclusion:_ Although conservative treatment is recommended as a first-line approach, the best conservative treatment cannot be determined with certainty due to the low to very low confidence in the results. 

#### **1. Introduction** 

Elbow stiffness is a common consequence following trauma or surgery, with an incidence ranging from 5% to 56% (Anakwe et al., 2011; Schrumpf et al., 2013), resulting in significant limb disability, psychological stress, and a negative impact on daily life (Liu et al., 2022; Siemensma et al., 2023). The functional range of motion to perform many ADL tasks requires a degree of elbow flexion higher than 130<sup>◦</sup> , such as the use of a mobile phone, computer mouse or keyboard, an elbow extension of less than 20<sup>◦</sup> , as opening a door or turning a steering wheel, and more than 50<sup>◦</sup> each of protonation and supination (Latz et al., 2019; Oosterwijk et al., 2018; Sardelli et al., 2011). As a result, patients’ functional range should be determined individually, considering their 

occupational and sport’s needs, hand dominance and strength, and should be considered while designing rehabilitation programs for patients (Jones, 2016). Elbow stiffness is defined as a loss of extension greater than 30<sup>◦</sup> , being the most common outcome, and a flexion of less than 120<sup>◦</sup> , which significantly affects arm functionality, since hardly compensated by other joints, with up to 12% of all elbow trauma acquiring a restriction in flexion that necessitate surgical approach (Akhtar et al., 2021; Murray et al., 2012; Myden and Hildebrand, 2011). The loss of motion after elbow injury can result from different intrinsic factors, such as intra-articular incongruity or adhesions, loose body osteophyte formation or malalignment of the articular surfaces, and extrinsic factors, as capsular collateral ligament and muscular contraction, as well as heterotopic ossification, although clinically it is common

In the post-traumatic elbow stiffness management, conservative treatment is recommended as the first-line approach, particularly within the first six months following the onset of stiffness (Siemensma et al., 2023; Yau et al., 2020). This is due to the considerable risks of post-operative complications associated with surgical release of a stiff elbow. However, despite the variety of approaches described for the conservative management of post-traumatic elbow stiffness, there appears to be limited evidence (Jones, 2016). 

The purpose of this systematic review is to investigate and compare the effectiveness of conservative interventions in patients with elbow stiffness of traumatic or postoperative origin. For the purpose of this study, effectiveness was measured as the improvement in function, pain, and range of motion. 

#### **2. Methods** 

#### _2.1. Study design_ 

The protocol of this systematic review was pre-registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO: CRD42024517823) and followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) statement (Page et al., 2021) and Cochrane guidelines (Higgins et al., 2019). 

#### _2.2. Search strategy_ 

The search strategy was conducted in PubMed (MEDLINE), Cochrane Library, CINHAL, Web of Science (WoS), Scopus, and Physiotherapy Evidence Database (PEDro) from inception to April 05, 2024. The following Medical Subject Headings were used in the search strategy: elbow, contracture, rehabilitation, conservative treatment, physical therapy modalities, and exercise. These terms were combined with other keywords and linked with the Boolean operators AND/OR. The search strategy used in each database is shown in Appendix A. A hand search of the reference lists of all the included studies was performed. 

#### _2.3. Screening and study eligibility criteria_ 

The studies identified during the searches were screened to remove duplicates. Two reviewers (AP and AZ) independently assessed titles and abstracts of the publications retrieved during the electronic search based on the inclusion criteria. Differences during the screening process were discussed between the two reviewers until consensus was reached. If no consensus was obtained a third researcher (IV) was consulted to make a final decision. 

The inclusion criteria were developed according to the Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, and Study design (PICOS) framework. All the studies met the following criteria: Population: subjects suffering from post-traumatic elbow stiffness; Intervention: conservative

#### _2.4. Data extraction_ 

Data were extracted using a standardized form that documents participants’ characteristics (number, age, gender), selection criteria, interventions (modality, frequency, duration), control characteristics, 

outcome measures, follow-up periods, and results. Two independent reviewers (AP and AZ) extracted data and any discrepancy was resolved by an independent third reviewer (IV). 

#### _2.5. Risk of bias assessment_ 

The risk of bias (RoB) assessment of the selected papers was performed by two independent assessors (AP, AZ) using the Revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB2) (Sterne et al., 2019). This tool rates each paper according to potential bias arising from five main domains: randomization process, deviation from intended intervention, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome and selection of the reported results. Judgments of each domain and overall risk of bias were based on an algorithm aimed to assign to each item a quality rating of low RoB, some concern or high RoB (Sterne et al., 2019). Disagreements were discussed between the two reviewers and any remaining disagreements were resolved through further discussion with a third researcher (IV).

#### _2.6. Data analysis and synthesis_ 

#### _2.7. Quality of evidence_ 

Quantitative data collection was based on sample size, mean scores, and standard deviations. Between-group effect sizes were calculated where feasible. For ROM, pain, and function measures, which comprised solely continuous data, Standardized Mean Differences (SMD) (Hedge’s g) were determined along with their 95% confidence intervals. Cohen’s criteria (Cohen, 1988) were used to interpret effect sizes, meaning that 0.2, 0.5, and 0.8 were used to determine small, medium, and large effect sizes respectively. We visually assessed evidence of publication bias through funnel plots. Review Manager Software (RevMan 5.4) was used to perform the statistical analyses. Heterogeneity was explored based on clinical characteristics, RoB of the studies, and visually assessed in the forest plot. Since high heterogeneity of interventions and comparators did not allow for a metanalysis to be performed, a structured narrative synthesis was provided based on intervention type and follow-up duration. 

The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) framework was used by two reviewers (AP, AZ) and, in case of disagreements, a third author (IV) adjudicated to assess the confidence in the body of evidence reviewed (Guyatt et al., 2008). This framework suggests rating levels of results across five domains (risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias) as high, moderate, low or very low (Guyatt et al., 2011). For each outcome, evidence was downgraded in accordance of the presence of the following: risk of bias (downgraded by one level if the study was at some concerns of bias; and two levels if the study was at high risk of bias), inconsistency (evidence was downgraded by one level when results were from one single study), indirectness (low risk of indirectness was attributed by default because all outcomes included were deemed appropriate concerning the specificity of populations, interventions, comparators, and outcomes guaranteed by the eligibility criteria), imprecision (downgraded by one level if the estimates comes from small sample studies) (Guyatt et al., 2011), risk of publication bias (downgraded by one level if there was suspected publication bias).

#### _3.4. Outcomes_ 

#### _3.4.1. Elbow functionality_ 

Elbow functionality was evaluated in all the studies examined by administering the DASH questionnaire. All studies provided quantitative data; however, due to high heterogeneity of interventions and comparators, and different follow-up timing, it was not possible to perform a meta-analysis. Effect sizes were calculated for each study and presented in Figs. 3–4, and GRADE analysis was presented in Fig. 7. In the short term, one study (Faqih et al., 2019) showed higher improvement at the end of the treatment in the group where MET was applied immediately post immobilization compared to the group where MET was started one week after post removal of immobilization (SMD = 3.07; 95%CI [1.91, 4.23]), but the quality of evidence was very low (downgraded for risk of bias, inconsistency and imprecision). Furthermore, large effect sizes were shown for PNF versus static stretching (Birinci et al., 2019) at the end of the treatment (SMD = 0.85; 95%CI [0.20, 1.50]), and after six weeks from discharge (SMD = − 1.22; 95%CI [− 1.90, − 0.54]), but the quality of evidence was low (downgraded for inconsistency and imprecision). In the long term, large effect size was observed in favour of GMI when compared to a structured exercise program (Birinci et al., 2022) involving active and passive movements of the upper limb (SMD = − 2.79; 95%CI [− 3.59, − 2.00]), although the quality of evidence was low (downgraded for inconsistency and imprecision). No differences were noted for other interventions in the short and long term. 

#### _3.4.2. Pain intensity_ 

Four studies (Bhosale and Kolke Pt, 2023; Birinci et al., 2019, 2022; Faqih et al., 2019) investigated the effectiveness of different conservative approaches on diminishing pain intensity. Effect sizes were calculated and represented in Fig. 5 while GRADE analysis was presented in Fig. 8. Moderate to large effect size was shown for GMI versus control 

(Birinci et al., 2022) for both pain at rest (SMD = 0.57; 95%CI [0.00, 1.14]), and pain during activity (SMD = 0.86; 95%CI [0.28, 1.44]) at the end of the treatment, and after 12 weeks from enrolment (SMD = 1.48; 95%CI [0.85, 2.12] at rest, SMD = 1.21; 95%CI [0.61, 1.82] during activity), although the quality of evidence was low (downgraded for inconsistency and imprecision). Moreover, moderate to large effect sizes were observed for PNF (SMD = 0.50; 95%CI [− 0.13, 1.13]) (Birinci et al., 2019), and MET (SMD = 1.83; 95%CI [0.91, 2.76]) (Faqih et al., 2019) in comparison to control groups at the end of the treatment, demonstrating a reduction in pain during activity and rest, respectively. However, the quality of the evidence was rated as low to very low. For the other comparisons against control, no differences were found. 

#### _3.4.3. Range of motion_ 

Four randomized controlled trials reported the efficacy of conservative treatments at discharge on flexion-extension ROM for posttraumatic elbow stiffness (Bhosale and Kolke Pt, 2023; Birinci et al., 2019, 2022; Faqih et al., 2019), while only three studies investigated the efficacy on pronation-supination ROM (Bhosale and Kolke Pt, 2023; Birinci et al., 2019, 2022). Effect sizes were calculated for each study and presented in Fig. 6. The subgroup analyses indicated that MET could significantly improve flexion-extension ROM when conservative treatment was started immediately after the immobilization period (Faqih et al., 2019), but no differences were found when compared to IASTM (Bhosale and Kolke Pt, 2023). Moreover, moderate and large effect size were shown in favour of PNF (Birinci et al., 2019) and GMI (Birinci et al., 2022) to improve flexion-extension ROM. When investigating the efficacy of conservative treatments on pronation-supination ROM, no differences were found among the proposed conservative treatments.

#### **4. Discussion** 

This systematic review aimed to summarize the available evidence for the effectiveness of conservative interventions in patients with elbow stiffness of traumatic or postoperative origin, in terms of improvement in function, pain, and ROM. Results indicate that conservative treatments suggested in the literature showed substantial differences in terms of techniques, dosage, timing and procedures applied, making them difficult to compare. Indeed, proposed conservative interventions range from soft tissue to splinting techniques, to instrumented assisted manual therapy or approaches more focused on activating the premotor and primary motor cortex via motor-cognitive strategies. Overall, results indicate that initiating conservative treatment early leads to improved outcomes in function, pain reduction, and ROM in the short term. 

Although the literature suggests that the majority of patients are likely to regain functional range of motion within a year following elbow trauma, it is widely agreed that prompt initiation of the rehabilitation process, whether active or passive, is a safe and effective method for restoring elbow function and facilitating a quick return to work activities, without increasing the likelihood of complications (Giannicola et al., 2014; 

Harding et al., 2011; Iordens et al., 2017; Jones, 2016; Siemensma et al., 

2023). Despite the etiopathogenesis of post-traumatic elbow stiffness is not fully understood, the literature indicates that an increase in the extracellular matrix occurs soon after trauma or surgery, leading to a subsequent contracture (Doornberg et al., 2014). Therefore, conservative strategies primarily ought to focus on treating local structures to impede this remodeling process and prevent the onset of elbow stiffness. Enhancing treatments with motor-cognitive strategies, rather than solely relying on passive approaches of local structures, has proven to be an effective way to alleviate pain and mitigate the adverse effects associated with immobilization in the short to medium term. While the literature lacks a systematic review of conservative treatment, three reviews assessed the efficacy of splints (Chen et al., 2017; Müller et al., 2013; Veltman et al., 2015). The analysis of the studies has revealed that only two papers have investigated patient satisfaction following conservative treatment (Birinci et al., 2019, 2022). Recent studies indicate that patient satisfaction is not solely dependent on treatment outcomes (Hush et al., 2011; Rossettini et al., 2020). Therefore, while enhancing clinical results is beneficial, it is not the only factor influencing patient satisfaction. An important factor that needs to be highlighted is that the clinical efficacy of therapeutic interventions is reliant on a complex interplay of multiple factors. It highlights that the outcomes of each therapeutic intervention are contingent upon the interplay among various factors, delineating a multidimensional construct shaped by individual patient/provider characteristics, as well as clinical and contextual factors (Liu et al., 2022; Rossettini et al., 2020). 

Although this review did not examine the effectiveness of conservative treatment in comparison to invasive interventions, it is important to recognize that the body of evidence which supports conservative treatment is less reliable than an invasive approach. Given the higher risk of complications associated with more invasive procedures, conservative management should be the standard first-line approach, due to the lower risk of complications (Araghi et al., 2010; Cai et al., 2015, 

2016; Cui et al., 2019; Iordens et al., 2017; Zheng et al., 2018). Nonetheless, recent research indicates that botulinum toxin injections during surgery may enhance functional recovery without increasing complication rates (Backer et al., 2021¨ ; Freibott et al., 2020). Consequently, future research should prioritize comparing conservative and invasive treatment outcomes, including patient satisfaction. Additionally, it is imperative to establish a standardized treatment protocol with defined follow-up durations and outcome metrics to facilitate effective comparisons and determine the most efficacious conservative treatment. 

#### _4.1. Strengths and limitations_ 

#### **5. Conclusion** 

Overall, the available evidence on the topic is limited. Despite conservative management should be adopted as the first choice of treatment in the management of post-traumatic elbow stiffness, the most effective conservative treatment in reducing disability, pain, and restriction of mobility could not be determined. Hence, it is advisable to carry out further high-quality research to explore which conservative methods, types, and dosages are most effective.
